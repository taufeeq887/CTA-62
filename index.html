The Issue

What is Call to Action #62?

Call to Action #62 is one of the 94 Calls to Action released by Canada's Truth and Reconciliation Commission. It calls on every province and territory to make Indigenous history — including the truth about residential schools, treaties, and Indigenous contributions — mandatory in every K-12 classroom across the country.

Right now, that is not happening. And the result is that millions of Canadian students are graduating without ever being properly taught one of the most painful and significant chapters in their own country's history. This is not just an Indigenous issue. This is a Canadian issue.

I chose this Call to Action because I was one of those students. I did not know the full story behind residential schools. I did not know the last one did not close until 1996. I did not know that over 150,000 Indigenous children were forcefully taken from their homes. That is not ancient history — the survivors are still alive. And the fact that I had to learn this through a school project instead of being taught it tells you everything about why Call to Action #62 exists in the first place.

Context

Three Things You Need to Understand

01

What Were Residential Schools?

Residential schools were government-funded, church-run institutions designed to forcibly assimilate Indigenous children into European-Canadian culture. Children were taken from their families, forbidden from speaking their languages, stripped of their names, and punished for practicing their culture. The curriculum was mostly manual labour. The goal, by the 1930s, was not education — it was replacement. The government called it "Christian Citizenship."

02

Why Was This Hidden?

Canada has spent decades building a reputation as welcoming, fair, and rights-based. The truth about residential schools directly contradicts that image. The same government that ran those schools for over 160 years is still controlling what gets taught in classrooms today. It was not a coincidence that this was left out of the curriculum. It was a calculated decision to protect Canada's image at the expense of the truth.

03

What Is Call to Action #62 Actually Asking For?

It is not asking for special treatment. It is not asking for guilt. It is asking for the truth to be taught — to every student, in every province, from every background. Equity means giving everyone access to the same accurate history. Call to Action #62 is about equal, fair, and complete education. No more, no less.

History

A Timeline of What Happened — and What Keeps Happening

1831

First Residential School Opens

The Mohawk Institute opens in Brantford, Ontario — the beginning of a system that will forcibly enroll over 150,000 children.

1876

The Indian Act is Passed

The Canadian government passes legislation giving itself legal control over nearly every aspect of Indigenous peoples' lives — land, education, culture, identity.

1920

Attendance Made Mandatory

The government makes residential school attendance mandatory for all Indigenous children aged 7–15. Parents who resist face criminal charges.

1930s

"Christian Citizenship"

The goal of the schools formally shifts from basic education to total cultural replacement. Children are described as products to be manufactured into "Christian Citizens."

1996

Last School Finally Closes

Gordon Residential School in Saskatchewan closes its doors. The system ran for over 160 years. The survivors — and the children of survivors — are still living with the effects today.

2008

Official Government Apology

Prime Minister Stephen Harper issues a formal apology on behalf of Canada for the residential school system.

2015

94 Calls to Action Released

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission releases its final report with 94 Calls to Action, including Call to Action #62 — mandatory Indigenous curriculum in K-12 schools.

2016–18

Ontario Commits — Then Cancels

The Province of Ontario commits to updating the curriculum with Indigenous studies in 2016. A new majority government cancels the initiative in 2018.

2020

Alberta Tries to Remove All Mentions

Alberta moves to remove residential school content from Kindergarten to Grade 4 curriculum entirely. The pattern of erasure continues.

2026

Call to Action #62 — Still Not Implemented

More than a decade after the TRC's report, mandatory Indigenous curriculum is still not the law of the land in most Canadian provinces. We are still here. Still waiting.

The Impact

What Happens When You Erase Someone's History From the Classroom?

🧠

Psychological & Social Harm to Survivors

Studies show that residential school survivors face significantly higher rates of psychological and social challenges than the general population. Trauma does not end when the schools close — it gets passed down through generations.

⚖️

Overrepresentation in the Prison System

Indigenous peoples make up nearly 80% of provincial custody admissions in Canada — while being only 10% of the population. This is a direct consequence of the intergenerational trauma and systemic neglect that the residential school system created. You cannot understand this statistic without knowing the history behind it.

📚

Indigenous Students Erased in Their Own Classrooms

When Indigenous children sit in classrooms and their entire history, culture, and language does not exist in the curriculum — the message is clear: you do not matter. That is not education. That is erasure happening in real time.

🇨🇦

All Canadians Are Left Ignorant

The need to know this history is not just for Indigenous students. Every single Canadian student deserves to know the truth about their country. Without it, we cannot understand why things are the way they are — and we cannot change them.

Do Something

Five Steps. No Excuses. No Money Required.

Step 01 · Weeks 1–2

Educate Yourself First

Before you advocate for anything, you have to know what you are talking about. Use the NCTR, CBC Beyond 94, and the TRC's 94 Calls to Action to build a foundation. If you are reading this page, you have already started.

Free · Takes: A few hours
Step 02 · Weeks 2–4

Share the Campaign

Use #TeachTheTruth. Share this website. Post the statistics — 150,000 children. 165 years. 80% of provincial custody admissions. Make it impossible to scroll past. The goal is not guilt. The goal is awareness.

Free · Takes: 2 minutes
Step 03 · Ongoing

Sign and Share Petitions

There are active petitions pushing the federal and provincial governments to make Indigenous curriculum mandatory. Signing takes 30 seconds. Sharing takes another 10. There is no easier step in this entire plan.

Free · Takes: 40 seconds
Step 04 · Month 2

Email Your School Board Trustee

Write an email to your local school board trustee asking where your board stands on implementing Call to Action #62. It is free, it is direct, and it puts pressure exactly where it belongs. If enough students do this, it becomes impossible to ignore.

Free · Takes: 15 minutes
Step 05 · Always

Hold Them Accountable — Don't Let Them Make You Forget

The government counts on people forgetting. Use the CBC Beyond 94 tracker to watch what provinces are doing — and what they are rolling back. Every time something happens, share it. The Indigenous voice was stripped away once. We are not going to let it be stripped away again by silence.

Free · Takes: Choosing to care
